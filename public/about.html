ABOUT THE PROJECT

IT careers have a reality
that numbers don’t show.

Behind the Paycheck is an independent platform for anonymous, employee-reported experiences across India’s IT job market and workplace. It focuses on applying for jobs, interviews, switching companies, offers, notice periods, salary growth and day-to-day work.

“

A job title tells you what someone does. It doesn’t tell you what they experience.

01

Why this exists

IT professionals may face unanswered applications, recruiter ghosting, unclear interview feedback, offer changes, notice-period conflicts, immediate-joining demands, salary negotiation problems, low hikes, delayed pay or unsustainable workloads. This project makes recurring job-search and workplace concerns easier to see through transparent aggregate counts and voluntary stories.

02

What this site is — and isn't

This is a reporting and awareness platform, not a job service, counselling service, financial service or legal service. It does not promise to resolve individual cases. Its long-term ambition is to help make employee concerns visible to employers, industry groups and policymakers through evidence that is clearly described and responsibly presented.

03

How the numbers work

  • Issue counts come from submitted reports where a contributor selected that issue.
  • A single report can select several issues and therefore count toward several categories.
  • Daily visit totals count page visits, not verified unique people.
  • Submissions are self-reported and are not automatically proof of a claim.
  • Visitors are self-selecting; results should not be treated as a representative survey of all Indian IT employees.
  • Reports can be duplicated or inaccurate. Counts should always be read with these limitations in mind.
04

Privacy by design

No account, name, email address, phone number, profile or salary-slip upload is required. The report form does not ask for your name, email, phone number, gender or age/experience profile. It stores the company you name, selected issue categories, story text, report status and submission time so a moderator can review the report and approved reports can contribute to aggregate trends. Between the two submission steps, the company name and selected categories are held temporarily in this browser tab’s session storage and removed after a successful submission. To reduce repeat spam, the browser creates a random anti-abuse token in session storage; the server stores only a SHA-256 hash of that token in a separate guard table. The token is a pseudonymous anti-abuse identifier, not a name or account.

Important: server hosting providers, reverse proxies, security services or infrastructure may independently generate technical logs. We have not independently verified all provider log settings. The operator must review and configure those services consistently with the privacy promise and applicable law. Do not include names, contact details, exact work locations, account numbers, confidential company material or any details that could identify you or another person.

05

Public submissions and safety

Submitted stories are public. Avoid posting identifying information, confidential information, threats, hate, or unsupported allegations about identifiable people. Public stories may be copied or saved by other visitors. Reports are reviewed before they can contribute to public trends. The anti-abuse token hash is cleaned up after 30 days when a later submission triggers cleanup. A scheduled retention policy for report content, a public-facing self-service removal workflow and a published support contact are not yet configured. Pending or approved report content may therefore remain stored until moderation or operator action. Do not submit information you may later need to retract.

06

Independence and impact

The goal is to make patterns visible, not to target an individual employee, employer or public official. Any report shared with companies, industry groups or policymakers should include methodology and limitations, suppress small or identifying details where necessary, and distinguish employee reports from independently verified facts.
